## Task1: How to read an Annual Report

Preparation and Approach
Define your purpose: Start by identifying why you are reading the report—whether for investment research, professional context, or academic study.

Choose your format: Use the version that works best for your workflow, whether it's an interactive online version, a PDF, or a hard copy for manual note-taking.

Set a time limit: Allocate one hour for your initial review to focus on the big picture and avoid getting lost in trivia.

Analyzing the Content (50/50 Split)
Narrative (50%): Start with the letter to shareholders to understand the CEO’s vision and strategy, then move to the business risks section to identify potential threats.

Numbers (50%): * Review the one-page financial summary for key performance indicators.

Income Statement: Use vertical scans for profit margins and horizontal scans for year-over-year growth.

Cash Flow Statement: Focus on the starting/ending cash balance and the inflows/outflows that drove the change.

Balance Sheet: Evaluate what the company owns (assets) versus what it owes (liabilities and equity) to understand the company's financial health.

Digging Deeper
Peeling the onion: If you need more detail, investigate specific line items using the Management Discussion and Analysis (MD&A) or the notes to the financial statements.

Fiscal Year Check: Always verify the reporting period. Companies often use different fiscal years (e.g., Microsoft's July–June cycle) instead of the standard calendar year.
